# Part 5: Key Observations

## 5.1. Model Ranking (Best to Worst)

1. **Model A (Lumped Series, \(A=1\))** – Average error 2.0 V
2. **Model B (Nominal π, \(A=0.954\))** – Average error 5.7 V
3. **Model C (Distributed, \(A=0.8667\))** – Average error 14.2 V

## 5.2. Trend Analysis

| Quantity | Observation |
|----------|-------------|
| **Effect of increasing L** | Measured \(V_r\) increases from 82→88→92 V. All models capture this trend correctly. |
| **Model A accuracy** | Best match across all tests. Error decreases as L increases (2.3→2.7→1.0 V). |
| **Model B error** | Consistently overestimates by 4-7 V. Error decreases slightly at higher L. |
| **Model C error** | Severely overestimates (14-15 V high). At 2.0 H and 2.8 H, predicts \(V_r > V_s\) (physically impossible for passive RL load). |

## 5.3. Physical Interpretation

The **inductive load** creates a lagging power factor. The parallel inductor:
- Provides a path for current that **reduces** the effective load impedance magnitude
- Increases the phase angle of \(Z_{load}\) (from purely resistive to inductive)

As \(L\) increases (less inductive current):
- \(Z_{load}\) magnitude increases (approaches 750 Ω)
- Voltage drop decreases → higher \(V_r\)

**Why Model A best matches:**
- With inductive load, the shunt capacitance in Models B and C creates a **parallel resonance** condition with the load inductance
- At certain frequencies (near 50 Hz), this resonance can cause voltage amplification
- Your physical trainer's discrete π sections have parasitic elements that **damp** this resonance, making the line behave more like a series-dominant circuit

## 5.4. Model C Anomaly

At 2.0 H and 2.8 H, Model C predicts:
- \(V_r \approx 102-107\ \text{V}\) from \(V_s \approx 101\ \text{V}\)
- This **voltage gain** (\(V_r > V_s\)) is impossible for a passive, lossy line

This confirms that the **distributed/telegrapher's model with nameplate capacitance is invalid** for your hardware. The effective shunt capacitance at 50 Hz is much lower than nameplate suggests, likely due to:
- Parasitic series inductance in capacitor branches
- Discrete π section approximation (3-6 sections) vs continuous line
- Component tolerances and aging
